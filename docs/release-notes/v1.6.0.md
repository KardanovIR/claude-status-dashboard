The last of the three clients is on the design system, the board has session
history, and the two platforms CI had never touched are built and tested.

## Android looks like the rest of the product

1.5.0 shipped the redesign to iOS and wrote down what it had not reached;
1.5.1 did the web board and the public pages. Android was the last one, and it
was still the palette this project names as its first anti-reference: a
near-black `#0B0D12` with six unmodified Tailwind defaults on it — `blue-500`,
`purple-500`, `amber-500`, `red-500`, `emerald-500`, `gray-500`.

`Theme.kt` is a **transcription** of the iOS table rather than a second
conversion of `tokens.css`. Three clients converting the same OKLCH values
independently is three chances to disagree, and the point of a design system is
that they cannot.

The palette was the easy half. What came with it:

- **The accent.** It was `planning` blue in 27 places — buttons, switches, the
  FAB, every link — with white or `textPrimary` on top of it, measuring 2.72:1
  and 1.9:1. It is `done` green now, because `done` is the state that hands the
  turn back to you, and anything filled with it takes `Theme.onAccent`: 9.41:1,
  where white is 2.05:1.
- **The card's 4dp edge stripe is gone**, replaced by the state tinted across
  the whole surface. That stripe is the most overused device in dashboard UI
  and never reads as intentional, whatever colour it is given — and a large
  tinted area reads from much further away than a 4dp sliver.
- **Nothing glows.** The blocked card carried a 12dp coloured shadow and the
  connection dot a 3dp one. A lit halo is the trading-terminal tell and it is
  exactly wrong in a dark room at 1am, which is when this board gets read.
- **`done` is no longer dimmed.** It was drawn at 0.55 alpha, from when it
  meant "finished, nothing to see". It means the agent is waiting on **you**.
  Only staleness dims a card now.
- **State is no longer carried by hue alone.** Every status has a mark as well
  as a colour, on the card, in the history header and down the timeline. Rough
  1 in 12 men cannot separate the red and green states by colour at all.
- **Nothing pulses.** The status badge animated forever on every active card:
  ambient animation that reported nothing, never stopped, and cost battery to
  keep saying the same thing. The three animations that do report something —
  the meter fill, the self-hosting disclosure, the list reordering — now honour
  the platform's animation scale, which the app ignored everywhere.
- **A meta line**: agent, machine, directory, when. The machine was only ever
  visible inside the Focus button's label, so two sessions of the same project
  on two machines were indistinguishable at a glance. It wraps, which the web
  board had to learn the hard way.
- **The Focus control got quieter.** Filled in the new accent it was the
  loudest thing on the card by a wide margin, and a card with two loudest
  elements has failed the first design principle. It is the same raised surface
  and hairline iOS uses.

Smaller things from the same pass: the pairing code's expiry clock was
`testing` teal and is `limitWarn`; the limit-history chart's series order
disagreed with both other clients; the project-token bar was a two-stop
gradient between two hexes belonging to no token; the launcher icon was eight
more of them; and the demo badge borrowed `coding` purple, which made the
toolbar look like a session in progress.

> **Still not published to any store.** The submission package in
> `distribution/google-play/` is updated for this release — listing copy,
> screenshots, versionCode — but the icon and feature graphic still need
> re-exporting from the new palette, and nothing has been uploaded.

## Session history on the web board

Tapping a card on the board opens that session's timeline: every status change
with its message and its time, newest first, at `#session/<id>` so it survives
a reload and a back button. The phone apps have had this since 1.0; the board
you leave open on the second monitor did not.

## Two platforms CI had never built

- **Android.** Every release until now shipped an Android client that nothing
  had compiled. It is built, linted, and — this is the part that matters — its
  five unit suites are finally run; the job built the APK and stopped. The
  debug APK is kept as an artifact for fourteen days, because the only machine
  that can build it is the runner: AGP 8.13 needs JDK 17.
- **The Windows installer.** `install.ps1` was parse-checked and never
  executed. It now installs for real on `windows-latest`, as a freshly created
  standard user, against a board built from the checkout — then upgrades over
  itself, and asserts what landed and that it runs. A second job asserts the
  argument guards refuse what they are supposed to refuse.

That second job found one on its first run: `-Bogus x` was answered with the
POSIX-flag message, advice about `--code` versus `-Code` for someone who never
typed a POSIX flag. A value starting with one dash is a misspelled PowerShell
parameter and now gets the list of parameters that exist. Both still refuse
before anything is downloaded.

## Fixes

- **The "·" between a card's facts failed contrast on iOS**, at 1.80:1 — the
  strong-hairline token used as a glyph. It is the same mistake the web audit
  caught at 1.52:1 and fixed on the web only. All three clients draw it in the
  tertiary text colour now, and the token says in all three what it is for.
- **iOS's `AccentColor` asset was still the retired blue.** The app's own
  palette had moved in 1.5; the asset the system tints with had not.
- **The plan-limit meters drew their track in white at 6%** rather than the
  surface token, so it was a colour belonging to nothing and drifting from the
  thing it was meant to be.
